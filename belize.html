Work in Belize

Building a nation's cultural infrastructure

Before the international missions, there was Belize. For twelve years as Director of the Institute for Social and Cultural Research at the National Institute of Culture and History, Nigel Encalada helped build the scholarly and policy foundations on which the country's cultural life now rests — research programs, publications, a national culture policy, and the institutional memory of a young nation.

His work reached from the archives to the classroom to the cabinet: editing the record of Belizean history and anthropology, drafting the policy that governs culture through 2026, curating national landmarks, and bringing the histories of slavery, emancipation, and independence to a broad public.

Public scholarship

History for everyone

Nigel is a recognized public voice on Belizean history and culture. He has served as a featured expert on 7News Belize for national coverage of the history of slavery and emancipation, published the ArcGIS StoryMap The documentary heritage of slavery and emancipation in Belize, and written widely for the public — including Culture in the time of Covid: A view from the Caribbean, carried on Belize's UNESCO living-heritage portal.

His research on Belize's peoples — the Kriol, the Garifuna, group positionality in a multiethnic society — continues to reach national and international audiences through conferences and media alike.
